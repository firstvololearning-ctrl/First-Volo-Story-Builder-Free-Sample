📖 About First Volo Story Builder — Free Sample

First Volo Story Builder is a research-informed narrative-language and story-building tool designed to keep the student's own story construction at the center. It combines visual story generation, explicit narrative-language targets, educator-controlled scaffolding, and a Whole → Part → Whole teaching cycle. It can support oral, written, typed, or scribed storytelling depending on the instructional goal.

Free sample: This version uses the same instructional framework, Story Goals, target-specific scaffold sequence, Tell Again routine, and student self-check as the full Story Builder. It includes a smaller visual-card collection and simplified saving so the actual teaching workflow can be tried without the full content and story-management features.

Welcome Story Instructional Support Planner Save Print Tips

👋 Welcome

Students generate visual story parts and decide how those parts fit together into a coherent story. The cards provide content to work with; they do not supply the causal or narrative relationships for the student.

Story Builder can be used for oral storytelling, planning, written or typed composition, or scribed composition. If handwriting, spelling, or typing is not the instructional target, an educator can scribe the student's language so those demands do not replace the language task.

🎲 Build a Story

Create a Story rolls the visual idea cards for Character, Setting, Problem, and Feeling. These give the student content to work with without deciding how the story should unfold.

The student then builds the rest of the narrative:

  • Plan: What does the character plan to do to solve the problem?
  • Action / Attempt(s): What does the character do to carry out the plan? If needed, what do they try next?
  • Resolution: What happens in the end? How is the problem resolved?

Plan is student-generated. If the student needs an idea, the existing Plan cards can be rolled as optional idea help. A rolled idea can be used, changed, or ignored; it does not replace the student's own plan.

Action / Attempt(s) and Resolution are student-generated. They are not rolled by the app.

Story path: Character + Setting → Problem → Feeling → Plan → Action / Attempt(s) → Resolution

The Item card is optional. It can add an object as an extra creative constraint, but Item is not treated as a required narrative function.

Because the app supplies only part of the story, the same visual combination can lead to many different plans, attempts, and resolutions.

⚙️ Customize Story Elements

Educators can show or hide story parts to match student needs. The core sequence includes Character, Setting, Problem, Feeling, Plan, Action / Attempt(s), and Resolution. Item is available separately as an optional story element.

🎯 Instructional Support

Story Builder organizes targeted narrative-language instruction around First Tell → Work on Target → Tell Again. The student begins and ends with a complete narrative, with focused teaching in the middle rather than turning the story into a series of isolated drills.

There are two ways to begin. If the instructional target is already known from an established goal, assessment, or prior performance, select it before the First Tell. Educator guidance can then focus listening on that target while the student's Story Goal remains hidden during the independent first attempt. If the target is not yet known, choose Observe First, listen to the complete first tell, and then select one primary target based on the most meaningful narrative or language breakdown.

During Work on Target, the selected target becomes explicit to the student through a brief Story Goal and a concrete look-for. The Story Planner is then used only where that target needs attention.

Educator controls and guidance are kept separate from student-facing language. The student attempts the language first. If support is needed, the educator selects Open support and then More support only as needed, making the scaffold progressively more explicit. After repairing or reformulating the language, the student retries the idea and reconnects the improved part to the surrounding story.

During Tell Again, targeted teaching support closes and the detailed look-for is removed. The student keeps only the shorter Story Goal in mind while telling the complete story again. The Story Planner may remain available as a visual/organizational scaffold or be hidden for a more independent retell.

After the retell, the student can complete a brief Yes / Sometimes / Not yet self-check about use of the Story Goal. This reflection is stored as a student self-report, not as an automated performance score.

Current instructional targets include Story Organization, Connections & Cohesion, Cause & Effect, Sentence Formulation, Elaboration, Perspective & Internal State, and Vocabulary Precision. Story Builder does not diagnose a target, choose one automatically, or score student performance.

Access help such as clarifying directions, locating a story element, rereading a prompt, navigating the planner, or explaining a non-target word is kept separate from support for the selected instructional target.

🗂 Interactive Story Planner

The Story Planner is an interactive graphic organizer. It carries the selected story ideas into a workspace and adds space for the student-generated Plan, Action / Attempt(s), and Resolution. It can support oral rehearsal, brief notes, scribed responses, or writing.

  • Character
  • Setting
  • Problem
  • Feeling
  • Plan
  • Action / Attempt(s)
  • Resolution

If the optional Item element is turned on, it also appears in the planner so the student can decide whether and how it matters to the plan, attempt, or other part of the story.

Guiding questions remain available as the planning baseline. When an Instructional Target is selected, targeted support can be opened one step at a time only where it is needed.

During a later retell or Tell Again, the educator may allow the student to refer to the Story Planner as a scaffold whether or not anything has been written in it. The planner may function through its story images and organization alone, through brief or scribed notes, or through student writing. Hide the planner when an unsupported whole-story tell is the goal.

💾 Save & Open Stories

Story Builder automatically saves the current work in this browser. Existing browser-saved work from the earlier free sample can still be restored.

Save Story downloads a Story Builder backup file. Open Story restores a saved Story Builder file. Older JSON save files from the free sample are also accepted.

Saved copies can preserve the story title, planner notes, writing, selected visual cards, display preferences, Instructional Target, Whole → Part → Whole stage, targeted-support state, and student goal reflection.

💡 Tips for Educators

Match the task to the instructional target

  • The student can tell the story aloud, type or write it, or dictate while an educator scribes.
  • If handwriting, spelling, or typing is not the target, do not require those skills to be the main way the student demonstrates narrative language.
  • Use only the story elements that are useful for the student and the instructional goal. Item remains optional, and a Plan Idea roll should be used only when idea-generation support is helpful.

Use targeted support responsively

  • Let the student attempt the language before opening targeted support.
  • Use the least support likely to help, then have the student retry the same language demand.
  • Reduce or remove support when the student can maintain the target with less help.
  • Provide access help when needed without treating it as support for the language target.

Look for transfer

  • After the student improves within one story, use a new story with the same target to see whether the skill carries over to new content.
